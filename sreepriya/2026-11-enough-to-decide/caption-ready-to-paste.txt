Look at the trail in this photo. You can see the next stretch clearly. The top is lost in the clouds.

Most important decisions look like this.

I value deep thinking. Reading, researching, questioning assumptions: that is where good judgement is built, and it should never be confused with speed.

But analysis has a point after which it stops adding clarity. And a decision doesn't go away when we delay it. It waits, and usually someone else carries the uncertainty while it does.

Some of us move once we feel more certain. Others prefer to act, learn and correct course along the way. Neither is wrong. The skill is knowing when you have understood enough to decide responsibly, and then having the courage to act.

We will rarely have 100% information or 100% certainty. What we can have is the judgement to assess the risk, the conviction to decide, the humility to admit when we are wrong, and the agility to change course without losing momentum.

You don't need to see the top of the trail. You need to see enough of it to take the next step well.

Which do you find harder: knowing when you have enough information, or acting once you do?

Save this for the next decision you are sitting on.
