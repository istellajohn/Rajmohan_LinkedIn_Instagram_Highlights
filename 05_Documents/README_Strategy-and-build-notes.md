## D. What the archive says

The volume says "family-office explainer". The sequence says something else.

1. **2010 to 2019: where do the wealthy put money.** He appears as a quoted voice on alternatives, portfolios and unlisted equity, at Kotak Wealth Management and then early Entrust.
2. **2017 to 2021: what a family office is for.** Thirty-four of the forty-seven authored pieces fall in these years (twelve in 2020 alone) and mostly teach the model: Entrepreneur India, Forbes India, Fortune India, Mint. This is the bulk of the archive and the most generic part of it.
3. **2017, quietly, a different argument.** The Forbes India essay "Zen and the art of wise wealth management" separates the furtively wealthy from the vulgarly wealthy and says India should return to its roots and be wise about wealth. That is the Wise Wealth thesis, published four and a half years before the book. Philosophy was there first; the book did not retrofit it.
4. **2022: the book,** with Lakshmi against Kubera as the governing image.
5. **2024 to 2026: he becomes the subject.** Mint, Economic Times, IndiSight and Asian Private Banker now write about how he thinks, not only what he says. The question has moved from where to put wealth to how a family carries it: continuity, governance, the discipline after success.

Three consistencies I found and would trust, because they cross years and publications: conflict-free advice (ET 2020 on Sebi norms; the 2026 IndiSight account of leaving the sell side), succession as design rather than reaction (Mint 2017 against CXO Digitalpulse 2025), and scepticism about concentration in property (Forbes 2017 against the Mint 2024 quote).

One inconsistency to handle openly: in 2014 he wrote that alternative assets are a risky class; in 2025 the firm is reported as adding them to push growth. It is not necessarily a contradiction (an adviser's caution about retail access is not a firm's institutional allocation) but it is exactly the thing a hostile reader would find. A Re-read that addresses it would be stronger than silence.

Two quieter threads. Ekatvam (founded in 2015 in memory of his father, per Business Today) is the only place the archive lets him talk about himself, and it is the human centre of the collection. And in September 2025 Entrust named Sreepriya as CEO with him continuing as founder and MD: the firm practised the succession it advises. There is no comment from him in that coverage, so it stays out of the Highlight, but it belongs in the story you tell.

The sentence the Highlight should quietly say: **wealth is easier to create than to carry well, and he has been saying so for nine years.** (The second half is checkable, 2017 to 2026. The first half paraphrases the IndiSight close and must be attributed as IndiSight's phrasing, never as his quote.)

The honest risk: forty-seven authored pieces, many of them explainers, can read as content marketing if shown at equal weight. Curation is what turns that into a body of thinking.

## F. Visual system (short form; full spec in `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`)

- **Type:** Newsreader (serif, display and italic) with Instrument Sans (tracked caps for metadata). Both SIL OFL 1.1, free for commercial use, from Google Fonts.
- **Colour:** the supplied Entrust palette as the spine (#610124 burgundy, #4F1228 wine, #A87B51 bronze, #CCB59F sand, #B7909F mauve, #ECC5D4 blush, #F7F7F7 ivory), with warm paper and charcoal added. Burgundy ground for contemporary Stories, warm paper for archival ones.
- **Signature device, the authorship meter:** three dots borrowed from the Entrust mark. Filled dots show how much of the piece is his own voice: three for written by him, two for conversation, one for a feature, none for a quote. A viewer learns the difference between an essay and a cameo without reading a word.
- **Folio:** RECORD / 016, small, top left. **Link zone:** dashed 920 by 190 box, centred above the bottom reply area. **Safe zones:** top 333 px and bottom 453 px at 2K.
- **Era:** 2019 and earlier are warm paper with grain, fibre and a bronze stamp. 2020 onward are burgundy, flatter and sharper.
- **Dimension:** floating paper sheets with real shadow, and one frosted slab. Nothing chrome, nothing glass-blob. Strip the shadows and each Story still holds.

## G. The five sample directions

All use real text and, where noted, real page captures taken on 12 Oct 2026.

1. **PAPER (M101, Mint, 2017, written).** The standfirst's own word, *yesterday.*, is the hero, cropped at the edge. The real headline block floats beneath it as a sheet. Archival.
2. **FRAGMENT (M002, IndiSight, 2026, featured).** One verified direct quote, set huge. "When I represent a bank, I represent an institution that wants to make profits." A thumbnail of the headline sits underneath as proof.
3. **COVER (M063, Business Today, 2020, featured).** The publication's portrait as a framed plate with the title and one verified quote about Ekatvam and his father. The only image-led direction.
4. **SLAB (M005, Economic Times, 2026, featured).** The page recomposed as a floating sheet over a frosted slab that carries the metadata. Note the headline is typeset in our serif (verbatim); masthead and synopsis are captures.
5. **DIVIDER (WRITINGS).** A fanned stack of three authored pieces from 2017 and 2025 with year stamps. Shows how section dividers can carry information (eleven from forty-seven).

My recommendation: **build on PAPER for archival Stories, FRAGMENT and SLAB for contemporary ones, COVER only where there is a genuinely good publication portrait, and the DIVIDER pattern as is.** They share chrome, so this is one system with four registers rather than four systems. If you want one pure direction, FRAGMENT is the strongest alone and the cheapest to keep consistent, but it is also the easiest for another executive to copy by swapping a name. The authorship meter and the real-page objects are what stop that.

Candid critique of my own samples: PAPER and DIVIDER show their article crops small, so the headline text is hard to read on a phone. In the full build I would crop tighter (headline only) or typeset the headline beside a smaller capture. The SLAB headline is typeset, not captured; if you prefer strict capture, I will crop it at native scale and accept a partial headline.
